# National Housing Bank — Presenter notes

Irfan Nazar · BJK22AR007

## 01. National Housing Bank

Open with the relationship between money and space. A housing proposal needs land, design, approvals, infrastructure and finance. This presentation asks where NHB fits into that process and whether its support produces homes that people can afford and use. Estimated speaking time: 15–18 minutes. The interactive activities are optional during a short classroom slot.

## 02. The institution behind housing lenders

Define refinance as financing supplied to a lender against eligible loans it has made. HFC means housing finance company. A primary lending institution, or PLI, deals with the household. NHB works mainly through this institutional layer. The distinction matters because NHB refinance eligibility and household loan approval are different decisions. Source detail: Annual Report 2025–26, printed pp.20 and 32–34. The FAQ contains old ownership and regulation wording. Only its statement on the absence of direct individual loans is used here.

## 03. Why housing needed an apex institution

Explain the difference between a housing need and bankable demand. A family may need a house but lack documented income, clear title or the savings for an initial contribution. NHB’s creation addressed institutional finance constraints. The final statement is architectural analysis: increasing credit alone cannot supply serviced land or guarantee an adequate dwelling. The institution emerged from the Seventh Five Year Plan review, followed by the Rangarajan-led high-level group and the 1987 legislative decision.

## 04. A changing mandate

Use the 2019 milestone to correct a common textbook error. Government ownership and the transfer of regulatory powers are separate changes in the same year. Regulation concerns the rules and registration of HFCs. NHB continues inspections and monitoring, as the latest annual report confirms. Do not describe NHB as the current licensing authority for HFCs. RESIDEX tracks residential prices rather than construction cost or the affordability of every household.

## 05. Inclusion with stability

The vision and mission are paraphrases rather than quotations. Stability matters because a lender that cannot meet its obligations cannot sustain credit delivery. For PMAY-U 2.0, EWS means household annual income up to ₹3 lakh, LIG above ₹3 lakh up to ₹6 lakh, and MIG above ₹6 lakh up to ₹9 lakh. These particular limits belong to that scheme. Other schemes and earlier policies can define the groups differently.

## 06. Governance and relationships

This is a functional summary, not a reproduction of every department or the official organisation chart. The Annual Report 2025–26, printed pp.20–25, describes governance and regional presence. National ownership does not remove state and municipal responsibilities for land-use permission, infrastructure and local implementation. NGO involvement is shown as a possible support relationship, not as a formal NHB administrative tier. Households generally connect through PLIs.

## 07. How NHB’s finance works

The table separates two financing relationships. Avoid presenting one universal NHB rate, collateral rule or tenure. Institutional security may include the relevant statutory security arrangements and scheme documentation. The underlying retail mortgage belongs to the PLI. Refinance can support liquidity, but a PLI still assesses repayment ability and clear title. In the 2024 scheme booklet, loans for furnishing, fees and insurance are also excluded. Resource mobilisation is covered in the latest annual report, printed pp.35–36.

## 08. Finance connects to housing through several routes

This is the core housing slide. Explain that NHB’s influence is usually indirect. It does not choose every plot or design every dwelling. Technology and materials enter through research, capacity building and green-housing support. NHB is not a construction-technology certification agency. Rural housing access also depends on income seasonality, title and the local lender network. UIDF urban services complement housing but its sanctioned projects cannot all be counted as houses.

## 09. Different instruments, different beneficiaries

Read one row across as objective, target group, mechanism, implementation and outcome. AHF uses institutional refinance, whereas ISS uses a subsidy credited through a PLI. UIDF supports infrastructure. SUNREF combined credit, investment support and technical assistance. The latest report, printed pp.40–44 and Annexure VIII, supplies the current data. Additional NHB initiatives include fixed-rate refinance, construction refinance, small-ticket rural loans and a slum redevelopment / low-cost housing scheme. RHISS has historically supported eligible rural households outside the relevant PMAY coverage. CRGFTLIH provides credit-risk support to eligible lenders for lower-income housing. Neither a guarantee nor a subsidy means automatic loan approval.

## 10. The household’s loan still needs to be affordable

Move the loan slider from ₹15 lakh to ₹20 lakh while holding the other values constant. This shows why a cheaper rate does not erase the price of land and construction. EMI is P × r × (1+r)^n / ((1+r)^n − 1), with r the monthly rate and n the number of months. The percentage uses a hypothetical monthly household income of ₹30,000, with no assumed universal lending threshold. ISS is not a flat 4% retail loan and the model does not calculate its benefit. MoHUA guidelines, printed pp.23–26, explain ISS and its conditions. Families must meet the housing-ownership and prior-assistance conditions as well.

## 11. Happinest Palghar-1

Use this case to connect finance policy and architectural choices. The diagram is the source’s developer rendering and should not be called a site survey or as-built plan. The developer’s 2018 results verify the launch month. The 2022 booklet documents its features and HDFC as a retail lender. Do not assert an NHB project-finance amount. Some fields in the booklet’s p.50 date table are misaligned, so they are not used. The case concerns the documented project scope, not every later phase under the same brand.

## 12. Compact homes and shared environmental services

Keep a critical tone. The case source describes green measures and buyer interviews, but those do not prove reduced bills for every resident. The source describes AAC blocks in one section and CLC blocks in the detailed envelope specification. Do not select one as verified without project drawings. The lesson for an architect is to coordinate an affordable unit with common services, accessible circulation and a maintenance plan. The discussion answer is an interpretation rather than a measured project outcome.

## 13. Refinance at national scale

Point to the difference between a flow and a stock. Disbursement is money released during a year. Outstanding is the balance still owed at a date. Cumulative refinance is a historical sum and is not the number of distinct homes constructed. The latest annual report gives exact AHF units in Annexure VIII. HFCs received about 80.4% of the year’s refinance. The financing network is an achievement, but social and architectural outcomes require additional evidence.

## 14. Credit access remains uneven

Separate the strength of an institution from the limits of its instrument. A larger refinance portfolio can coexist with exclusion. For sustainability, consider energy and water systems alongside access to jobs and everyday services. Administrative challenges include coordination, reliable documentation and monitoring. Financial challenges include repayment risk and interest-rate movements. The independent SUNREF evaluation gives a concrete example of weak borrower pass-through and monitoring, discussed on slide 16.

## 15. The architect’s contribution

These are professional implications, not a list of compulsory NHB submission documents for every scheme. DPR means detailed project report. Scheme compliance depends on the selected programme, approvals and applicable local requirements. Explain lifecycle affordability using construction cost, EMI, utility bills, maintenance and transport. A specification for efficient equipment should include how residents or the association can maintain and replace it. Technology should fit local capability and budget.

## 16. SUNREF’s evaluation tests the success claim

This is the strongest evidence-based critical slide. AFD’s project evaluation distinguishes activities from attributable effects. The programme achieved revised participation targets, but that does not show the programme caused those patterns. Its environmental claim was difficult to attribute because properties already existed. The 2.4% measure relates to the evaluation’s applicable interest-rate cap, not to all forms of support. The concluding assessment follows the evaluator’s recommendations for market-sensitive financial design, coordination and measured outcomes. Avoid applying the programme percentages to the Palghar project.

## 17. NHB’s value depends on what finance delivers

Close with a judgement, not a repeat of the definition. NHB is necessary to housing-finance infrastructure, but finance should be evaluated against its effects on residents. The practical professional response is to connect financing eligibility and cost control to quality, location and post-occupancy evidence. Use the check question only if time permits. A likely viva question is whether NHB can solve the housing shortage by itself. Answer that it works through a wider system of lenders, governments, public agencies and developers.
